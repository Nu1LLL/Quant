## Economic mechanism and evidence limitation

Native units newly created by proof-of-work monetary policy dilute existing holders and may become miner sales. **Prediction:** the coin with the *lowest* recent gross issuance relative to outstanding supply outperforms the one with the highest over the subsequent week. This is a cross-coin *monetary supply* hypothesis, separate from the preceding BTC miner-hashprice/exit-stress rule (which used BTC dollar revenue divided by hash rate and a hash-rate decline), and from DeFi protocol-revenue, TVL, exchange-inventory, price trend and funding signals. It is not guaranteed statistically independent of those returns. Coin Metrics defines `IssTotNtv` as [gross total native issuance](https://docs.coinmetrics.io/network-data/network-data-overview/supply/supply-issuance), not net issuance after burns or unlocks of already-issued tokens; `SplyCur` is outstanding supply. This signal **does not measure treasury unlock dilution** or actual miner sales.

The paper [*Token Dilution and the Cross-Section of Cryptocurrency Returns*](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=6636258), posted April 2026, reports a dilution premium in broad crypto but finds it absent in mature coins. Our three older proof-of-work assets are therefore a deliberately low-prior transfer test, not a replication of that paper's young-token mechanism. The paper also postdates the test years, so publication date supplies no independent executable OOS. Historical Coin Metrics observations are today's reconstructible vintage; historical first-published values and real order-book fills are unavailable. Evidence grade is `preregistered_retrospective_current_network_vintage`; `independent_executable_oos=false` regardless of numerical results.

## Frozen signal, orders and account

At each Wednesday 00:00 UTC, the most recent admissible network day is the preceding Sunday `s`. For each coin compute `score = (365/28) × sum(IssTotNtv over s−27..s) / SplyCur(s)`. The denominator and all 28 issuance days must be present and positive. Lower score is the predicted winner. Sort ascending by `(score, symbol)`, **long the lowest, short the highest**, middle flat; no threshold or score standardization. Sunday labels are treated as available no earlier than Monday; Wednesday decision and Thursday fill leave a further publication buffer. Actual historical release timestamps remain unverified. There are exactly **260** scheduled Thursday 00:00 UTC executions, 2021-01-07..2025-12-25. At each Thursday open, close both legs and open the newly ranked pair **only if the pair changed**; otherwise retain quantities without turnover. Force-close at 2026-01-01 00:00 open.

Start at **10,000 USDT**, no external contributions or cash yield. Each new pair allocates 50% of then-current equity notional to each leg (1x gross at entry), round both quantities down to **0.1 coin**, require each entry/exit leg ≥10 USDT. Positive funding paid on the long and received on the short at each archived 8-hour event, using the prior completed hourly close; fund old positions at 00:00 before rebalancing, including the terminal 2026-01-01 event. Every fill costs **5 bp taker fee** and **10 bp adverse slippage**: buy/cover at open×1.001, sell/short at open×0.999. A proposed leg above **1% of its preceding 30 complete days' median daily futures quote volume** seals capacity failure. That is a coarse capacity proxy, not a fill guarantee. Mark every hour, use simultaneous long-low/short-high adverse bar marks for conservative drawdown; if equity ≤0 or gross market notional/equity >**5x**, seal execution failure rather than inventing a favorable liquidation. Reconcile cash, quantities, funding, fees, slippage and realized PnL; missing bars/events seal source failure.

Run the same rankings in three precommitted comparators: zero fee/slippage (actual funding retained), double slippage **20 bp per fill**, and one extra day of latency (Friday 00:00 entry, same terminal close). Both stressed accounts must end above 10,000. No scenario is selected after seeing results.
