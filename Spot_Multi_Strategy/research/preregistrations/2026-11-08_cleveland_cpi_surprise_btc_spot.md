## Mechanism, novelty and evidence grade

The [Federal Reserve Bank of Cleveland](https://www.clevelandfed.org/indicators-and-data/inflation-nowcasting) publishes business-day headline CPI month-over-month nowcasts and charts that show historical forecasts alongside the first available actual release. Its stated standard update time is about 10:00 Eastern; BLS typically releases CPI the following month. The fixed hypothesis is that a **lower-than-nowcast headline CPI print** relaxes risk discounting and causes **positive seven-day post-release BTC drift**. An equal or higher print means cash. A contemporaneous macro shock can also be fully priced within minutes, so this is a falsifiable transfer hypothesis, not a known profitable rule. Pinchuk's [inflation-announcement study](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.10117) reported an adverse BTC announcement-window response to positive inflation surprises but did not establish this exact post-announcement spot drift.

The full tracked-document novelty screen covered **311** prior preregistrations and sealed reports with the phrases CPI surprise, inflation surprise, nowcast, Cleveland Fed, 通胀预期差, 通胀意外 and 公布后漂移. It found only mentions in the **real-yield** preregistration and the prior **CPI disinflation-state** preregistration. That earlier strategy compared two realized twelve-month inflation growth rates and held BTC for a month; it used **no forecast or release innovation**. This experiment tests a distinct economic input, although macro risks and realized returns can correlate. No earlier result for the exact signal has been read.

Evidence grade: `preregistered_retrospective_historical_nowcast_chart`; `independent_executable_oos=false`. Today's Cleveland Fed JSON is a reconstructed historical chart, not our contemporaneously recorded forecast journal; the chart is said to display the **first available actual** but the exact archived first-publication file and fillable quote are not independently held.
