## Mechanism and evidential limit

The hypothesis is that an exceptional increase in Bitcoin's pending on-chain settlement backlog reflects broad transfer/transaction demand that may spill over into subsequent BTC spot demand. The [Blockchain.com chart methodology](https://www.blockchain.com/explorer/charts/mempool-size?timespan=all) describes its mempool series as transactions waiting for confirmation, aggregated from its own nodes. This is distinct from price momentum, stablecoin supply, miner hashprice, Treasury/Fed liquidity, exchange inventory and perpetual turnover. Mempool congestion can also arise from inscriptions, spam, fee policy or transfer bottlenecks without investment demand; the sign is unproven. No second signal, coin, model or cross-source ranking is permitted in this experiment.

The [official Charts API](https://www.blockchain.com/ru/explorer/api/charts_api) exposes fixed `start`, `timespan`, `sampled`, and `rollingAverage` parameters. Evidence grade `preregistered_retrospective_current_vintage_blockchain_com_mempool`; **`independent_executable_oos=false`**. Historic chart values may have been revised or processed after their nominal timestamp. A 48-hour observation buffer reduces publication latency risk but cannot prove point-in-time historical availability. The 2024–2025 holdout is retrospective, not five years of genuinely future OOS. No actual orders.
